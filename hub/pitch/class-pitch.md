# Take The Next Step — How We're Running This Project

Hey everyone. I want to show you something I put together for us and explain the thinking behind it.

**Live now:** https://glenn-lewis-hub.github.io/webd3100-team-hub/

---

## The problem we'd have without this

Five teams. Five components. All of them depend on each other to produce one finished app. Without a shared view of where things stand, here's what typically happens on projects like this:

- You don't know if the team you depend on is on track until it's too late
- Blockers stay hidden until they become crises
- Meetings spend half the time on status updates instead of decisions
- At Week 9 when we switch components, the incoming team has no idea what state things are in

I've seen this play out. I didn't want it to happen to us.

---

## What I built

A coordination hub — a live dashboard that shows every team's status in one place. It's already running. Your team gets a card on it the moment you send me a raw URL to a small JSON file in your repo.

No new accounts. No new tools to learn. You update one file, push it, and the hub reflects it within seconds.

---

## What the hub gives us

**A progress feed** — post updates any time with a timestamp. Finish something at midnight? Post it. The team sees it in the morning. You don't have to wait for a meeting to communicate momentum.

**A team board** — a message space per team so quick questions don't get lost across Teams, Discord, and in-person conversations. One place, always there.

**Meeting minutes built in** — decisions get logged as they're made. Nobody loses track of what we agreed on three weeks ago.

**Blockers at the top** — if you're stuck, flag it in your status. It goes to the first item on the next meeting agenda. No blame, no pressure — just a conversation about what you need to keep moving.

**Bill can see it too** — our progress is visible to the instructor live, without anyone having to chase us for updates or write separate reports.

---

## The rules we're running on

These are simple, and I want everyone to have a say in them. This is a starting point, not a decree.

| Thing | How we handle it |
|---|---|
| Updates | Weekly minimum, any time welcome |
| Blockers | Top of meeting agenda, solution-focused |
| Done | Working + documented + accessibility checked + handoff-ready |
| Disagreements | Both sides post a video, class votes — last resort only |
| Accessibility | Check in with Edward at each milestone, not just the end |

---

## What this looks like in the real world

What we're running is a lightweight version of how professional development teams actually work. Status tracking, async updates, defined handoffs, escalation paths for disagreements — these are real practices. Jira does this at scale. We're doing it lean, for a five-team class project, with tools we already know.

When this project is done, every one of us can point to this and say: I worked on a coordinated multi-team build with a structured handoff process. That's a real thing to say in a job interview.
